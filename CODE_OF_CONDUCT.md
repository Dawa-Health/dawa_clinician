# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Dawa Clinician brings together engineers, clinicians, designers, device and hardware engineers and community members from different countries, languages and professions. We want everyone to be able to take part without harassment or disrespect, whatever their age, gender, ethnicity, nationality, language, religion, disability, health status, sexual orientation, level of experience or profession.

This Code of Conduct applies to everyone who takes part in the project: maintainers, reviewers, contributors and anyone who opens an issue or comments.

## Where it applies

- This repository: issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and commit messages.
- Project email, calls, meetings and events.
- Clinic visits, field testing and device testing done for the project.
- Any public place where someone is representing the project.

## Expected behaviour

- Be respectful and assume good intent.
- Give feedback on the work, not the person. Explain what should change and why.
- Respect expertise of different kinds. A clinician's judgement about safety and workflow, a device engineer's knowledge of the hardware and a reviewer's concerns about data protection all carry weight alongside software judgement.
- Many contributors write in a second or third language. Do not mock spelling, grammar, accent or code-switching.
- Discuss pregnancy, cervical screening, and sexual and reproductive health in a factual, professional way.
- Use synthetic or de-identified data, and images you have the right to use, when you test a change or describe a problem.
- Accept correction gracefully, and correct others kindly.
- Credit other people's work, including clinical review, device work and QA.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, threats or stalking.
- Insults, personal attacks, or demeaning comments about a person or group.
- Sexual language, imagery or advances directed at anyone. Clinical discussion of sexual and reproductive health, including cervical screening, is not a breach.
- Stigmatising language about people because of a health condition, pregnancy, pregnancy loss, HIV status, marital status, sexual history or reproductive choices.
- Remarks about a patient's body or appearance, in images or records, that go beyond what the clinical work needs.
- Sharing anyone's private information without their permission.
- Sharing patient, user or other personal health information in any form. This includes patient records, encounters, appointments, screening results, screening and ultrasound images, screenshots of the app, exported records, logs and test data.
- Using access gained through the project, such as a clinician, administrator or service-role account, or a clinic's devices, to view or copy data you are not authorised to see.
- Knowingly submitting false or unsafe health content, making a stale, fallback or unverified screening result look confirmed, weakening Row Level Security or other protection of health data, or submitting content you do not have the right to share.
- Repeatedly disrupting discussions, or continuing behaviour after being asked to stop.

## Reporting

If you experience or see behaviour that breaks this Code of Conduct, email developers@dawa-health.com.

Please include:

- what happened, and where (a link if there is one);
- who was involved;
- when it happened;
- anything else that would help us understand it.

You do not need to have been the target of the behaviour to report it.

If your report involves personal or patient information, tell us where it is rather than copying it into your email.

If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first.

Security vulnerabilities, such as exposed keys, gaps in Row Level Security policies or a way for one role to see another's records, are not Code of Conduct reports. Report them privately to developers@dawa-health.com, not in a public issue.

## How reports are handled

- Reports are read by the project maintainers. We aim to acknowledge a report within five working days.
- The identity of the person reporting is kept confidential and is shared only with those who need it to handle the report.
- If a report concerns a maintainer or reviewer, that person takes no part in handling it. You can say in your email that you want the report to go to Dawa Health leadership instead of the maintainers.
- We will hear from the people involved before deciding what to do, unless that would put someone at risk.
- Nobody will be penalised for making a report in good faith.

## Consequences

Maintainers decide on a response in proportion to what happened. Possible responses are:

- A private note explaining the problem and what is expected, for a minor or first issue.
- A formal warning, with conditions such as staying out of a discussion for a period.
- A temporary ban from taking part in the project.
- A permanent ban, for serious or repeated breaches.

Maintainers may also remove, edit or reject comments, commits, issues and other contributions that break this Code of Conduct.

Sharing personal or patient information is treated as serious in every case. The content is removed as soon as it is found, including from the repository history, issue and pull request attachments, and the QA screenshots in `docs/qa/` where possible.

## Appeals

A person who disagrees with a decision can ask for it to be reviewed by emailing developers@dawa-health.com within 30 days. The review is done by someone at Dawa Health who was not involved in the original decision.

## Changes

Changes to this Code of Conduct are proposed by pull request and must be approved by a maintainer.
